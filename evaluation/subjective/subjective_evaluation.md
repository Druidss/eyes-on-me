# Evaluation

## Study overview

This evaluation summarizes the Game Experience Questionnaire (GEQ) and the main mixed-method findings for 19 participants. GEQ responses were collected on a 1–5 scale and converted to the official 0–4 component scale by subtracting 1 before calculating each component mean. The results are descriptive and should not be interpreted as population estimates.

## GEQ component scores

| GEQ component | N | Mean (0–4) | SD | Median |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Competence | 19 | 2.34 | 1.06 | 2.60 |
| Sensory & imaginative immersion | 19 | 2.75 | 0.59 | 2.83 |
| Flow | 19 | 2.47 | 0.76 | 2.40 |
| Tension / annoyance | 19 | 0.68 | 0.68 | 0.67 |
| Challenge | 19 | 1.85 | 0.66 | 1.80 |
| Negative affect | 19 | 0.47 | 0.68 | 0.00 |
| Positive affect | 19 | 2.91 | 0.68 | 3.00 |

SD indicates how much participant scores varied around the mean. The median is the middle score after ordering all participant scores.

### Interpretation

- **Positive affect was the strongest dimension** (M = 2.91), suggesting that participants generally found the experience enjoyable.
- **Sensory and imaginative immersion was moderately high** (M = 2.75), indicating that the scene and gaze-based interaction supported engagement.
- **Flow was moderate** (M = 2.47), suggesting focused involvement, although divided attention and usability constraints sometimes interrupted the experience.
- **Competence and challenge were moderate** (M = 2.34 and M = 1.85), showing that the game was manageable but still required effort.
- **Tension/annoyance and negative affect were low** (M = 0.68 and M = 0.47). The game created suspense without producing broadly negative emotional responses.

## Key insights

### 1. Divided attention creates meaningful tension

Balancing eye contact, evidence inspection and dialogue memory creates the main source of suspense and challenge. This trade-off is engaging, but unclear goals or dense information can make players overly cautious or reduce story comprehension.

### 2. Tracking is trusted, but usability constraints can disrupt immersion

Participants generally trusted gaze-tracking accuracy. Calibration, posture requirements and the visible gaze cursor occasionally distracted players or made them monitor their own movement instead of focusing on the scene.

### 3. Suspicion feedback is too subtle and ambiguous

Many participants did not notice or fully understand the suspicion feedback. Because the relationship between gaze behaviour and suspicion was unclear, some players avoided exploration and adopted overly conservative gaze strategies.

### 4. Scripted opportunities strengthen immersion and suspense

Moments when the NPC turned away or answered the phone created clear opportunities to inspect evidence. These moments produced relief, urgency and a stronger sense of covert agency, making them the most effective recurring interaction pattern.

## Overall evaluation

The gaze-driven mechanic successfully creates suspense by forcing players to divide their attention between social eye contact and evidence gathering. The experience is generally positive and moderately immersive. Its main limitation is not the tracking accuracy itself, but the clarity of goals and suspicion feedback. Clearer onboarding, more legible state changes and robust calibration could improve usability without removing the tension that makes the mechanic distinctive.
